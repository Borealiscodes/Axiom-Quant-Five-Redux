\section{Holonomy Transport Operator}

\subsection{Overview}

The holonomy transport operator defines the continuation of altitude A8
by incorporating the manifold's connection structure into the embedding
pipeline. Whereas the curvature envelope modulates the spectral
projection using local geometric information, holonomy transport
captures the accumulated geometric effect of moving through the
manifold.

This section formalizes holonomy transport, its construction, and its
role in the Redux geometric pipeline.

\subsection{Levi-Civita Connection}

Given the metric tensor $g$ on the manifold $M$, the Levi-Civita
connection $\nabla$ is uniquely defined by the properties of being
torsion-free and metric-compatible:
\[
\nabla_u v - \nabla_v u = [u, v],
\]
\[
u(g(v, w)) = g(\nabla_u v, w) + g(v, \nabla_u w).
\]

The connection determines how vectors are transported along curves in
the manifold.

\subsection{Parallel Transport}

Let $\gamma : [0,1] \to M$ be a smooth curve. A vector field $V(t)$
along $\gamma$ is said to be parallel if
\[
\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}(t)} V(t) = 0.
\]

Parallel transport defines a linear map
\[
P_{\gamma} : T_{\gamma(0)} M \to T_{\gamma(1)} M,
\]
which preserves inner products and encodes the geometric effect of
moving along $\gamma$.

\subsection{Holonomy Group}

The holonomy group at a point $x \in M$ is defined as
\[
\mathrm{Hol}(x)
    = \{ P_{\gamma} \mid \gamma \text{ is a loop based at } x \}.
\]

This group captures the accumulated curvature of the manifold. In
particular, if the manifold is flat, the holonomy group is trivial.

\subsection{Holonomy Transport Operator}

Given a curvature-modulated vector $c \in \mathbb{R}^{80}$ and a point
$x \in M$, the holonomy transport operator is defined as
\[
h = P_{\gamma_x}(c),
\]
where $\gamma_x$ is a canonical curve determined by the manifold's
geometry. In the Redux runtime, this curve is chosen to reflect the
local geometric structure encoded in the metric tensor.

The operator $P_{\gamma_x}$ is represented by an orthogonal matrix
\[
H(x) \in \mathrm{O}(80),
\]
so that
\[
h = H(x) c.
\]

\subsection{Stability Properties}

Because parallel transport depends smoothly on the connection, we have
\[
\| H(x + \delta x) - H(x) \|
    \leq C \| \delta G \|,
\]
for some constant $C$ depending on curvature bounds. This ensures that
holonomy transport does not introduce instability into the embedding
pipeline.

\subsection{Altitude Interpretation}

Holonomy transport completes the geometric modulation at altitude A8 by
incorporating the manifold's connection structure. Whereas curvature
envelope captures local geometric behavior, holonomy transport captures
the accumulated geometric effect of moving through the manifold.

\subsection{Output}

The output of the holonomy transport operator is the transported vector
\[
h \in \mathbb{R}^{80},
\]
which serves as the input to the topology modulation operator in the
next stage of the pipeline.
