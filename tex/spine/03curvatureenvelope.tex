\section{Curvature Envelope Operator}

\subsection{Overview}

The curvature envelope operator defines the transition from altitude A7
(spectral geometry) to altitude A8 (curvature-modulated geometry). Its
role is to extract a normalized curvature signature from the manifold
and apply it as a modulation factor to the spectral projection vector.

This section formalizes the curvature envelope, its construction, and
its stability properties.

\subsection{Curvature Tensor}

Given the metric tensor $g_x$ at a point $x \in M$, the Riemann
curvature tensor is defined by
\[
R(u, v)w
    = \nabla_u \nabla_v w
    - \nabla_v \nabla_u w
    - \nabla_{[u,v]} w,
\]
where $\nabla$ denotes the Levi-Civita connection associated with $g$.
The curvature tensor encodes how the manifold deviates from flatness.

\subsection{Curvature Envelope Definition}

The curvature envelope operator extracts a normalized curvature
signature
\[
\kappa(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{80},
\]
defined by
\[
\kappa_i(x)
    = \frac{1}{Z}
      \left(
        \sum_{j,k}
        \left| R(e_i, e_j)e_k \right|
      \right),
\]
where $\{e_i\}$ is the standard basis of $\mathbb{R}^{80}$ and $Z$ is a
normalization constant ensuring
\[
\|\kappa(x)\|_\infty \leq 1.
\]

This signature captures the aggregate curvature behavior along each
coordinate direction.

\subsection{Curvature Modulation}

Given a spectral projection vector $s \in \mathbb{R}^{80}$, the
curvature-modulated vector is defined as
\[
c = s \odot (1 + \alpha \kappa(x)),
\]
where $\odot$ denotes elementwise multiplication and $\alpha$ is a
scaling constant (typically $\alpha = 0.1$ in the Redux runtime).

This modulation incorporates geometric curvature into the spectral
representation.

\subsection{Stability Properties}

Because the curvature tensor depends smoothly on the metric tensor,
$\kappa(x)$ is a smooth function of $x$. Therefore,
\[
\| \kappa(x + \delta x) - \kappa(x) \|
    \leq C \| \delta G \|,
\]
for some constant $C$ depending on the curvature bounds of $M$.

This ensures that curvature modulation does not introduce instability
into the embedding pipeline.

\subsection{Altitude Interpretation}

The curvature envelope operator lifts the representation from altitude
A7 (spectral geometry) to altitude A8 (curvature-modulated geometry).
This transition incorporates geometric information that cannot be
captured by spectral decomposition alone.

\subsection{Output}

The output of the curvature envelope operator is the modulated vector
\[
c \in \mathbb{R}^{80},
\]
which serves as the input to the holonomy transport operator in the
next stage of the pipeline.
